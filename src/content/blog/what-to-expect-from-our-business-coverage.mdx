Business reporting is most useful when it explains more than a price movement or corporate announcement. Readers also need to know who made the decision, what incentives shaped it, and how it could affect workers, customers, and communities.

## Following the decision

Our coverage starts with the people and institutions responsible for a development. We examine public filings, direct statements, available data, and the wider market or policy setting before drawing conclusions.

## Explaining practical consequences

A company story can affect employment, household costs, competition, public policy, or local investment. We identify those connections while separating confirmed outcomes from projections.

## Remaining independent

Commercial stewardship does not determine daily editorial decisions. Reporting choices are guided by relevance, evidence, and reader value.
