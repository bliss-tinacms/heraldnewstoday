A useful news tip gives the newsroom enough information to understand what may have happened and where verification can begin.

## Start with the essential facts

Tell us what happened, when and where it occurred, who was involved, and why the information matters. If the event is still developing, say which details are confirmed and which remain uncertain.

## Include supporting material

Documents, public records, photographs, links, or contact information for knowledgeable sources can help reporters evaluate a tip. Explain how you obtained any material you submit and whether there are restrictions the newsroom should understand.

## Protect sensitive information

Do not send confidential personal information through an ordinary web form. If your tip involves sensitive records or personal risk, use the first message to describe the situation generally and request a safer follow-up method.

Use the Contact page to send your message. Clear, specific information makes it easier for the newsroom to review and route it.
